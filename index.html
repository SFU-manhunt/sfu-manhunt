SFU Manhunt is a campus-wide game of hide-and-seek on Burnaby Mountain. Hiders and up to 3 seekers play on their iPhones, the play area shrinks over time, and every hider wears a wrist device we built with an Arduino.

How a round works

  1. Lobby: the host creates a game, players join with a 5-letter code, and the host picks up to 3 seekers.
  2. Hiding: hiders get a head start to scatter across campus.
  3. Hunt: the seekers use pings to find hiders and tag them in person.
  4. End: the seekers win by catching everyone. The hiders win if anyone survives the clock.

Game features

  • Live GPS map: every phone shares its position in real time.
  • Pings: every 3 minutes the seekers see all hiders for 30 seconds (the dots fade out).
  • Faster pings: if nobody is caught for 12.5 minutes, pings come every minute. After 15 minutes they come every 30 seconds until someone is caught.
  • Shrinking zone: an SFU-shaped border closes in stages toward a final point. Stay outside too long and you're eliminated (with GPS-error buffers so it's fair).
  • Off-limits areas: roads and forest edges drawn on the map for safety.
  • Tagging: a seeker tags a hider within 30 m, and the hider confirms on their phone.
  • Safety: the host can stop the game for everyone and send them to a meeting point.

AI-placed powerups (hiders)

  • 🛡️ Shield: you're hidden from the next ping.
  • 🎭 Decoy: the next ping shows you wherever you choose.
  • 🧭 Border Pass: leave the shrunken zone for 90 seconds (but not the original border).
  • The placement AI scores dozens of candidate spots and picks the best one: inside the zone, away from the seekers, about 120 m from hiders so it's worth the risk, and spread out near campus landmarks.

The wrist device

  • Hardware: Arduino UNO R4 WiFi, Grove Base Shield, 16×2 I2C LCD, button and buzzer, in a 3D-printed case on a strap.
  • Screen: shows the game stage, when the ring closes and for how long, and how many players are left.
  • Buzzer: buzzes when the ring starts closing, when a hider is caught, and when a ping reveals you.
  • Button: tap to choose a powerup, hold to use it, with no need to take out your phone.
  • Connection: it connects through the wearer's iPhone hotspot and syncs its clock with the game server.

How it's built

  • Game app: a web app (HTML/JavaScript) that runs in Safari on any iPhone, with nothing to install. Hosted on GitHub Pages.
  • Real-time backend: Firebase Realtime Database with anonymous sign-in and security rules.
  • Map: Leaflet with OpenStreetMap, using a custom SFU Burnaby campus outline and landmarks.
  • Device firmware: Arduino C++ over HTTPS (WiFiS3, ArduinoHttpClient, ArduinoJson).
  • Case: a parametric CAD model generated in the browser, with an automatic fit check for every part. Open the 3D case designer
